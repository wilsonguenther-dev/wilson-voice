Alright, this is going to be a long one, so bear with me. I want to talk through the plan for the next six weeks, the things that worry me, and what I need from each of you. I am recording this instead of writing it because I think faster out loud, and the app should be able to keep up with me. If it cannot, that is a bug, and you should tell me.

Let me start with the big picture. We have a product that people actually use every day now. That changes how we work. Before, we could break things and fix them the next morning and nobody would notice. Now, if dictation stops working on a Tuesday afternoon, someone is sitting in a meeting with nothing written down. So the first rule for the next six weeks is simple. We do not ship anything that makes the core loop slower or less reliable. Hold the key, talk, let go, and the words show up. Everything else comes second.

Second, speed. Short notes are already fast. The place we lose people is the long take, the three or four minute brain dump where someone talks through a whole idea while walking around the room. Right now that kind of take can sit there for several seconds after the key comes up, and that wait feels much longer than it is. I want us to measure it properly. We have three standard recordings, a short one, a medium one, and a long one. Every time we change the decoder, the windowing, the voice detection, or the cleanup, we run all three and write the numbers down. If the long one gets more than twenty percent slower, the change does not go in until we understand why.

Third, accuracy. Speed means nothing if the words are wrong. Each of those standard recordings comes with the text that was actually spoken, so the bench can tell us the word error rate next to the timing. If we make the decoder twice as fast and it starts dropping every tenth word, that is not a win, it is a regression wearing a nice number. I want both columns in the same row so nobody can quietly trade one for the other.

Fourth, the meeting side. People are recording calls, and the notes that come out of those calls are getting better. But the pipeline is heavier than dictation, and it competes for the same engine. We need to make sure a meeting finishing in the background never makes a dictation wait. That is a scheduling problem more than a speed problem, and I would like one person to own it end to end.

Fifth, and this is the one that worries me the most, memory. The app is meant to sit in the menu bar all day. If it slowly grows from a few hundred megabytes to a few gigabytes over a week, people will notice their fans before they notice anything we built. So every long recording in the bench should also tell us how much memory the process used at its peak. We do not have to be perfect, but we have to know.

Now, what I need from each of you. Please run the bench before and after anything you touch in the audio path, and paste the two rows into your pull request. Please do not tune the standard recordings to make your change look good. If you think a recording is unfair, say so and we will add a new one beside it instead of replacing it. And please, when something gets slower, write down why, even if the reason is that it is now correct.

That is it for now. Thanks for listening to all of that. Let us talk on Thursday and see where the numbers landed.
